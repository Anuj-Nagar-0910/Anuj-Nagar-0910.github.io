Experiment 03

Global IT Spend in Power BI

How does a large company keep $900M of IT spend honest? This dashboard compares plan, forecast and actuals across business areas, countries and cost types, and shows where the variance comes from.

Source on GitHub
Period
Built Oct 2026
Stack
Power BI Desktop, DAX, Power Query, Python notebook
Data
93,729 rows, 7 business areas, 30 countries
Context
Crio.Do DA-201, session 7
QQuestion

Where do plan, forecast and actuals drift apart?

  1. Cost distribution: how spend splits across 7 business areas, 5 IT areas (38 sub-areas), 6 regions (30 countries) and 5 cost-element groups (23 sub-groups).
  2. Budget and forecast alignment: where are the largest gaps between actual, forecast and plan, over time and geography?
  3. Structural drivers: what drives deviations in areas such as internal versus external labour, shared services and R&D planning?
MMethod

Power Query prep, a clean model, DAX measures

  • Append and tag. Combine the two sheets (81,610 + 12,119 rows) and tag each with a dataset period, so a single click can return to the original view.
  • Replace nulls with 0. Each ERP row fills only one of actual, forecast or plan. Dropping rows with nulls would erase the budget.
  • Sort months properly. Add a month number and quarter, and sort by column, so Power BI doesn't order months alphabetically.
  • Keep the credits. 1,463 negative actual rows (about −$126M) are valid chargebacks and accrual reversals. Removing them would inflate net spend.
  • Measure. Variance is defined once as Actual − Plan, so negative means underspend, and used everywhere.

A Python EDA notebook sits alongside the dashboard for checks that are easier in code.

RResult

Full-year actuals land 3.77% under plan

  • $900.40Mfull-year plan
  • $890.54Mforecast
  • $866.41Mactual
  • −$33.99Mactual vs plan (−3.77%)

The drill-downs are where the questions live: variance by business area and region, internal versus external labour, and a clear flag on Europe shared services overspend.

CCaveat

Plan is a full year; actuals are not

The first version of the numbers looked alarming: actuals 38% below plan. That is a comparison of eight months of actuals against twelve months of plan. The fair like-for-like view is January to August only:

  • Use the month slicer, not the dataset-period filter, for a fair year-to-date comparison.
  • Replacing nulls with 0 is safe here only because each row fills exactly one measure.
  • This is a practice dataset, so the real-world causes of the variances are not known.
