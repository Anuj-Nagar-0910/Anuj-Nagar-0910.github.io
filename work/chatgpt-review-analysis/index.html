Experiment 02

ChatGPT Review Analysis

A star rating shows how much users like an app, not why. The review text holds the why, but nobody can read 197,000 reviews by hand. So I taught Python to read them, and checked where it was wrong.

Source on GitHub
Period
Built Oct 2026; reviews Jul 2023 to Aug 2024
Stack
Python 3.12, pandas, TextBlob, NLTK, seaborn, scikit-learn
Data
196,727 raw reviews, 193,148 after cleaning
Context
Crio.Do DA-201, session 5
QQuestion

What do users feel about ChatGPT, how strongly, and about what?

  1. What is the general sentiment? (polarity: positive, neutral, negative)
  2. How strong are the opinions? (subjectivity: 0 factual to 1 opinionated)
  3. Which features do people praise or criticise most?

The data: Google Play reviews of the ChatGPT Android app, from 25 July 2023 to 23 August 2024.

MMethod

Clean, score, validate, then look for themes

  • Prepare. Standardise column names, drop 6 empty reviews and 3,573 duplicate review IDs. Duplicate text stays: two people who wrote "good" are two votes.
  • Score. TextBlob polarity and subjectivity. Positive above +0.05, negative below −0.05. Chosen because it returns both numbers, is fast and is explainable.
  • Validate. Compare sentiment with star ratings, study the mismatches, and check non-English reviews separately.
  • Mine. Top words and two-word phrases for 4–5★ and 1–2★ reviews, keeping negations so "not working" stays distinct.
  • Aspects. Share of praise and criticism mentioning errors, login, pricing, accuracy and helpfulness.
RResult

People like it. They are annoyed by reliability and access, not by the AI

  • 193,148reviews after cleaning
  • 4.51 / 5average rating
  • 76 · 21 · 4% positive, neutral, negative
  • 0.25rating vs polarity (Spearman)

Praise is about usefulness: helpful, best ever, helps a lot, user friendly. Criticism is mostly about reliability and access:

Stars and text disagree more than you would expect: the correlation is only 0.25, and about 1 in 8 low-star reviews use clearly positive wording (probable mis-taps, mixed reviews, typos such as "Nott good").

CCaveat

A lexicon-based model has blind spots

  • TextBlob misses sarcasm, slang and context, and the median review is only 4 words. About 20% of reviews score exactly 0.
  • It is English-only. Reviews with emoji or other languages land in "neutral" about twice as often (about 35% against 18%), which inflates the neutral share.
  • Aspect detection uses simple keyword rules, so the percentages are a lower bound.
  • One platform and about 13 months of data.

Ideas for further work: transformer-based multilingual sentiment, language detection, topic modelling on 1–2★ reviews, and linking review spikes to release dates.
